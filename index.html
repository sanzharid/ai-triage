EU AI Act: AI System Triage Tool

This tool helps provide a preliminary indication of whether a system might be considered an "AI System" under the EU AI Act. It is based on the definition in Article 3(1) of the Act.

1. Is the system a machine-based system?

This means it relies on physical hardware and software components to operate, rather than being a purely manual process or a set of rules on paper.

2. Is the system designed to operate with varying levels of autonomy?

This means it can perform tasks or make decisions with some degree of independence from direct, constant human control or intervention. Systems fully controlled by humans at all times are generally not considered autonomous in this context.

3. May the system exhibit adaptiveness after deployment?

This refers to self-learning capabilities that allow the system to change its behavior or improve its performance while in use, potentially producing different outcomes from the same inputs over time. This is a "may" condition and not strictly required for all AI systems.

4. Does the system operate for explicit or implicit objectives?

This refers to the underlying purpose or aim the system is designed to achieve.

  • Explicit objectives are specific goals directly defined and programmed by developers (e.g., "classify this image as a cat or a dog," "translate this text from English to French," "achieve a 95% accuracy in detecting manufacturing defects").
  • Implicit objectives might be less directly stated but are pursued by the system as a result of its design and learning processes (e.g., a recommendation system's implicit objective to "maximize user engagement" by learning what content keeps users on a platform, or a game AI's objective to "win the game" through learned strategies not explicitly programmed for every scenario).

5. Does the system infer, from the input it receives, how to generate outputs?

"Inference" here means the system determines its outputs using techniques like machine learning (e.g., neural networks, decision trees, statistical modeling) or complex logic-based approaches (e.g., knowledge graphs, reasoning engines). This is distinct from systems that operate *solely* based on pre-defined, explicit rules (e.g., "if X, then Y" coded directly by humans without learning/adaptation), simple data processing, or basic mathematical calculations.

6. Does the system generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions?

Consider if the primary function of the system is to produce these kinds of outputs that can then be acted upon or inform further actions.

7. Can these outputs influence physical or virtual environments?

This means the system's outputs (predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions) have the potential to cause effects or changes in the real world or in digital spaces.

Disclaimer: This tool provides a preliminary, simplified assessment based on the EU AI Act's definition of an AI system. It is not a substitute for legal advice. The classification of an AI system can be complex and depends on specific details and context. Consult with a legal professional for definitive guidance on the EU AI Act.
